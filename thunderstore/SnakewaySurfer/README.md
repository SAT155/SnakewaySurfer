# SnakewaySurfer

Waiting at the longhouse for your friends? Open the console (F5) and type **`surfsup`**.

Your viking auto-runs down the Snakeway, a three-lane track through every biome in order:
Meadows, Black Forest, Swamp, Mountains, Plains, Mistlands, Ashlands and Deep North (which
never ends). You dodge each biome's monsters, jump over fallen logs, slide under hanging logs
and flying monsters, and grab coins. You run a little faster with every biome, and the road
rolls more and more. Monsters use their own attacks: fast ones charge at you, slow ones walk,
and archers, mages and stone throwers shoot down the track toward your lane (slide under the
shot or change lane). In the Swamp you splash through shallow water.

In the Black Forest, watch out for trolls: one beside the track swings at its lane just as you run past, and another jumps down straddling the track with
its feet in both outer lanes, so you have to slide between its legs.

Once in each stretch of the Black Forest the track runs into a **burial chamber**: a stone gate
with two torches burning outside, then dark stone halls seen from above, lit by the torch in
your hand and a few sconces on the walls. The halls turn sharp corners: as you reach one, a
torch burns on the wall ahead and the camera starts to swing round, so press A or D toward the
opening to take it (you can still make it a moment late; leave it any later and you run into
the wall, which costs a life). Skeletons crowd the halls, archers among them, bone piles raise
more out of the floor as you come, ghosts drift in through the walls at head height (slide
under them), and fallen pillars and stone beams block the lanes. The old chests are worth ten
coins.

Once in each stretch of the Swamp it runs into a **sunken crypt** the same way, built from the
game's own sunken crypt walls and floors, with green fire burning at its gate. Draugr and blobs
fill its halls, grave mounds raise draugr as you come, and mud piles lie in the lanes to jump.
Some of its halls are flooded: slower going, and leeches swim in the water. Iron gates hang
over some halls and crash down as you come, with blue marks on the floor under them: roll
under.

Once in each stretch of the Mountains it runs into a **frost cave**: rough rock walls from the
game's own frost caves, lit by crystals glowing cold blue where the crypts have torches, with
blue fire at the cave mouth. Ulvs hunt in packs, fenrings leap, cultists breathe fire down the
lanes and bats flit under the roof (slide under them). As you come, icicles crack loose over the
lane you're in and fall, with a mark on the floor: get out from under them or jump the spike
they leave.

Once in each stretch of the Mistlands it runs into an **infested mine**: the dvergr's black
marble halls, lit by their standing lanterns and overrun. Seekers and their soldiers fill the
halls, egg sacs split open as you come and seekers crawl out, crates lie in the lanes to jump
and old timber beams to slide under. Ticks wait in the roof: as one lets go a red mark shows on
your lane, so get out from under it or jump it when it lands.

The Black Forest's burial chambers take turns with **troll caves**: shorter, of rough mossy rock
lit only by blue cave mushrooms and your torch, with greydwarfs about and one troll waiting in
the dark of the longest hall. As you come round into that hall it roars, the cave shakes and
rocks come down over your lane (get out from under them or jump what lands); then it comes for
you.

Once in each stretch of the Ashlands it runs into a **charred fortress**: grausten halls lit by
lava lanterns, held by charred warriors, their archers and mages, and twitchers. Spawners raise
more of them as you come, spiked beams hang across the lanes to slide under, flametal gates drop
as you come (roll under them), and channels of glowing lava run across some halls: jump them.
(The Ashlands come after the Mistlands, so set Loop Biomes to 7 or more to reach them.)
Dungeons in the config turns them all off.

Now and then one of the game's **traders** stands at the edge of the track in front of their
camp, lit by a lantern: Haldor in the Black Forest, the Bog Witch in the Swamp, and Hildir in the
Meadows once you're on the 1-star lap or later. They greet you as you come, and a line of coins
and a gold mark lead along the lane on their side. Run past them in that lane and they trade:
your lives back to full, or one extra if you already have them all (the lives on the HUD glow
gold). The lanes around them are kept clear. Traders in the config turns them off.

Each biome's own **places** stand beside the track now and then, the game's own locations as they
are in the world: abandoned houses, stone circles and ruined towers in the Meadows; tower ruins,
stone houses and greydwarf camps in the Black Forest; draugr huts and sunken ruins in the Swamp
(with its crypt entrances and fire spouts); graves, drake nests and towers in the Mountains;
standing stones, tar pits and fuling camps in the Plains; giant skeletons, dvergr towers and
swords in stone in the Mistlands; charred ruins and morgen holes in the Ashlands. Some have one
of their own waiting in the lane by them. Places in the config turns them off.

In the open Ashlands some rows are **lava** instead: a glowing channel across the track to jump,
or a pool along one or two lanes to keep out of (jumping in only lands you in it), with glowing
cracks running through the ash beside the track. Lava in the config turns it off.

## Bosses and checkpoints

Each biome ends with its boss, but only bosses you've already beaten in this world: a new
world's run stays in the Meadows (looping with stars), and each boss you kill in the real game
opens the next biome. Some coins become the boss's summoning item, in the game's own amounts
(2 deer trophies, 3 ancient seeds, 10 withered bones, 3 dragon eggs, 5 fuling totems, 3
sealbreaker fragments). You stay in the biome until you hold them all, then the boss fights
you: every attack marks the lanes it will hit first. To hurt it, grab its weapon from the track
(surtling cores, maces, frost arrows, bombs): the boss closes in and swings at your lane up to
three times, and parrying a swing (block just before it lands) staggers it: you get a chain of
blows in with the weapon in your hands, then roll back away. Eikthyr's spears
you throw yourself (left mouse): the spear flies down your lane and he weaves between the lanes
behind you, so throw when he's lined up. **Moder** flies ahead of you and spits shotgun volleys
of ice that grow into crystals and burst a moment later (each row leaves a lane free), then
lands to breathe frost down two lanes and rake the track with her claws. While she flies, grab
the obsidian arrows: the run stops, the Draugr Fang is in your hand, and you aim with the mouse
through the game's crosshair; hold to draw (the circle closes and turns yellow) and let go to
loose. Arrows fly and drop as in the game and hit her wherever their path goes through her.
When she's down, grab the silver atgeir and fight her up close. Three hits
wins and saves a checkpoint for the next biome. `surfsup` starts at your latest checkpoint;
`surfsup swamp`, `surfsup 3` or `surfsup plains 1` (biome, then stars) picks another one.

**Eikthyr chases** you: the camera swings round to look back at the boss running after you, and
its attacks come up the lanes from behind or land where you are. A red band rides along in the
lanes it's aiming at (at least a second's warning), then the blow lands with the boss's own
effects and sounds: Eikthyr's lightning, the Elder's roots bursting from the road, Bonemass's
poison, Moder's breath, Yagluth's fire and meteors. Waves and bars race up behind you, its creatures run past in two lanes, and Eikthyr
lowers his antlers and charges right up your lane. Left and right follow the screen while it
faces back. The Elder's roots even split the path into five lanes for a moment: find one
without red before they burst up. Each fight starts like at the altar: the summoning items rise
and circle, the altar's sparkles play, and the boss forms out of them. The arena takes on the
weather of the boss's own place, from Eikthyr's thunderstorm (with lightning and thunder) to
Moder's snowstorm.

Each arena has its boss's own place from the game looming on the horizon (Eikthyr's altar, the
Elder's stones, Bonemass's sunken tomb, Moder's peak, Yagluth's ruins, the Queen's gate), rising
out of the ground as you arrive. The path flattens, the land rises into walls on both sides,
torches in the boss's colour line the way, and the light and fog take on its colour.

**Fader** waits at the end of the Ashlands, in a lap that reaches them (Loop Biomes 7 or more)
and once you've beaten him in the world: collect three bell fragments, then dodge rocks falling
from the burning sky, walls of flame, and lava he pours down your lane or across the track
(keep out of the pools, jump the channels). Grab the blade on the track to strike him.

After the 3-star lap comes the **boss rush**: every boss in a row, with no items to collect and
no checkpoints. Beat the usual six (Eikthyr to the Queen) in one rush for the **Golden Surfer Shoes**, a trinket with a
winged emblem: +15% movement speed, and at full adrenaline another 5% plus 2.5x stamina regen.
You only get them once per character, and only players with the mod can see them. Don't drop
them on a shared server, because players without the mod can't load them.

## The ocean crossing

Between the Mountains and the Plains you cross the sea. The track runs down a beach into the
shallows, where a longship waits stern to the shore with open water either side; you leap
aboard at the helm and the camera rises above and behind the ship, low enough to see the swell,
ahead always up the screen. Steer with the rudder (A / D) and set the
sail with W / S, as in the game: oars (slow but steady, and the tightest turns), half sail or
full sail. The wind never stops, and now and then it turns (a gust, and the wind's icon swings
round the gauge by the stamina bar). The gauge is a ring round the ship's wheel, as in the game:
the wind's icon rides the ring on the side the wind comes from, in a dark arc that marks the
headings a sail can't drive on, and a pale tick on the ring marks your bow. As in the game, a
sail is fastest with the wind from the side, a little slower with it behind, and useless
heading into it: keep the bow's tick out of the dark arc (it turns red there) and tack across
a headwind. The icon is bright when the wind fills your sail and dark when it
doesn't. Holding sprint puts the crew to the oars as well.

At sea the blows land on the **ship**, not on you: the planks over the gauge are her hull. A
blow cracks one (a heavy one two), she sits lower and lists as the hull fails, and only when
it gives way does it cost a life (the crew patches her up to half). Driftwood mends two planks.
Three small islands lie at the channel's edge, the first a third of the way over, with a
workbench on the shore and a golden ring on the water around it: steer into the ring and take
in the sail (oars), and the ship moors while the hull is mended plank by plank. Raise the sail
to go on.

The bow ballista (left mouse) fires along your heading. The first stretch off the Mountains
coast is calm: sea stacks, loot and leviathans, and further out drakes come down from the peaks
to spit at where you're going (a red ring on the water). Past the first island the serpents
come, weaving in under the surface. A serpent either rears up beside the ship and lunges along
a red streak (only a parry stops a lunge), or comes up well ahead, stops dead and rears while a
wide red streak runs from its jaws toward you, then breathes freezing spray down it: steer out
of the streak (a block takes the spray). It's open to the ballista while it rears. Leviathans drift across your way until a bolt makes them shake and dive, sea stacks stand in the
channel, and barrels, chests and driftwood float by: sail through them for coins, and driftwood
mends the hull (two planks, or a life back when she is whole). Gulls wheel overhead.

Past the second island the sky darkens into a storm. Big rolling waves come with the wind:
meet them bow or stern first and you surf down their backs, but side on they roll the ship. A
pale band on the water shows the safe way to meet the next one, and it turns red while you're
side on. Lightning strikes on red rings. In the heart of the storm the **serpent of the deep**
rises: it circles the ship, lunges, slams the sea into a wave at you, and comes up from under
the keel (a red ring follows the ship; get off it), and stops to breathe a wide streak of spray. Hit it with the ballista while it's above
the water. Once it's beaten the storm clears, the Plains' deathsquitos come out over the
shallows, and the ship runs up the far beach; you run the length of the deck, leap off the bow
and carry on.

Beating Moder saves a checkpoint at the crossing, and the start menu gets an **Ocean** button
before the Plains; making the crossing opens the Plains themselves (`surfsup ocean` starts there
too). Ocean Crossing in the config turns it off.

Beside the track you pass the biome's own things: greydwarf nests and bone piles near their
creatures, fuling totems, Dvergr crates and lanterns, and the plants, mushrooms and stones you'd
pick up there.

To everyone else, you're just running in place at the longhouse wearing a Dverger circlet.
Friends without the mod see that too.

## Controls

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| A / D or ← / → | Change lane |
| W, ↑ or Space | Jump |
| S, ↓, Ctrl or C | Dodge roll under what's marked blue (in the air: drop fast and roll as you land) |
| Shift (hold) | Sprint: faster, uses stamina, to get past an attack before it lands |
| Right mouse (hold) | Block a creature's attack (stamina per blow and while held; you run a little slower, handy in the Elder's maze) |
| Right mouse just before a hit | Parry: free, and the attacker staggers |
| Left mouse | Throw a spear you picked up (Eikthyr); at sea, fire the bow ballista |
| Mouse, left mouse (hold) | Moder's bow: aim, hold to draw, let go to loose (Space or the arrow keys work too) |
| A / D at sea | Rudder: steer left and right |
| W / S at sea | Set the sail: oars, half sail, full sail |
| P | Pause and carry on |
| Space (after a crash) | Run again |
| Esc | Stop and go back to the world |

Stamina (the yellow bar at the bottom, shown when it isn't full) recharges after a second of
not sprinting or blocking. Without enough for a blow, the guard breaks and the hit gets through.
Logs, gas and tar can't be blocked.

The marks on the lanes tell you what to do without any text: **red**, get out of that lane;
**amber**, jump it; **blue**, slide under it. The summoning item shows as its icon with a count
at the top, and in a fight the boss's health bar takes its place. Getting hurt flashes the
screen red with your character's own hurt sound, blocks and parries ring with your shield's
sounds, and bats in the Swamp come in swarms that slow you and take some stamina but no life.
(Popup Text in the config brings back the old messages.)

Your character can't move or use hotkeys while you play. If something hurts you in the real
world, the game stops straight away (health lost because food is wearing off doesn't count).

The minigame has its own sounds (countdown, footsteps, jumps, slides, coins, new biomes,
crashes), borrowed from the game and heard only by you.

## Rewards

At the end of each run:

- **Run XP**: real Run XP, half of what running in the world for the same time gives (Run
  XP Per Second 0.5; at most 300 seconds count). Higher levels need more XP, as in vanilla.
- **One random food from the furthest biome you reached**, if the run lasted at least 30 seconds
  and you haven't had food from the minigame in the last 30 minutes (real time, saved on your
  character). With full bags, it drops at your feet.

## Config

Everything is in `BepInEx/config/com.snakesquad.snakewaysurfer.cfg` (or Configuration Manager):
biome length, speeds, jump and slide, camera, the reward amounts and cooldown, and for each
biome its monsters, flyers, scenery, logs and foods. Monsters and foods are named by the game's
own text keys (`greydwarfbrute`, `carrotsoup`), so you can add any creature or item. Names the
game doesn't have are skipped and logged.

## Multiplayer

Client-only. Nothing is spawned in the world: the track is built from lifeless copies of game
objects that only your own camera can see. The running animation and the circlet go through the
game's normal syncing, so they work on a vanilla dedicated server, with or without Valheim Plus,
and for players without the mod.
